# Research limitations

These limitations apply to the preserved frozen study and the separately versioned research-grade audit. New computations improve transparency; they do not retrospectively create untouched test data. Evidence and priorities appear in [RESEARCH_AUDIT.md](RESEARCH_AUDIT.md), with completed experiments distinguished from proposed work in [EXPERIMENTAL_RESULTS.md](EXPERIMENTAL_RESULTS.md).

## Information boundaries and independence

The 49 original episodes are a **retrospective** sample. Repository history records evaluation-period inspection before the controller freeze. Schedule-designated development block 6 is excluded from scoring, but its targets share five scored h1 outcomes with block 7, comprising fifteen cross-horizon target pairs. Keeping block 6 out does not restore independent confirmation. Neither deleting inconvenient episodes nor rerunning the same observations would resolve prior exposure.

A separate calibration/development defect affects the earliest development decisions. Calibration ends at origin 15 October 2018 10:50, whose final target is 11:50; development episode 1 begins at 11:00. Six decisions contain 21 affected horizon-decision cells and 56 repeated unavailable-label/decision pairs. At the first decision, 21 calibration labels have targets equal to or later than that decision. These are distinct counts, not alternative estimates of the same quantity. The frozen selection history remains preserved and qualified.

Operational residual causality nevertheless passes: all 42,336 reconstructed evaluation margins use targets strictly before their decisions and match the frozen module exactly. All fourteen saved feature columns across 50,125 origins reconstruct from current/past measurements. Current artifact consistency cannot prove the historical absence of test influence or attest an unrecorded training run. See the [forecast audit summary](../results/research_grade_v1/forecast_uncertainty/audit_summary.json).

## Data, forecasting and uncertainty

One turbine-year, four late-year contiguous segments and 49 retained daily episodes cannot establish generality across sites, seasons, turbines or forecast regimes. Exact acquisition version, licensing, timestamp timezone, measurement aggregation and latency require author verification. Complete-case selection and omitted gaps/tails may change the represented operating conditions. Raw forecasting features retain signed power; physical evaluation generation is clipped at zero. This convention is explicit, rather than a claim that negative readings are measurement errors.

XGBoost has lower aggregate RMSE but higher MAE than persistence at every horizon. Outcome-defined rapid-change strata favour XGBoost, but these are offline diagnostics, not available predictors or independently selected confirmation sets. The parameter-free local trend is weaker and reaches 17,510 kW because a defensible rated-power cap has not been established. It is a transparent baseline, not a physically bounded production forecaster. No model is retrained or selected using these comparisons.

Overall adaptive lower-bound coverage of approximately 95.9–96.1% conceals rapid-event coverage of 80.68% at ten minutes and 89.89% at sixty minutes. About 22.5% of target observations are zero; clipping a lower bound to zero contributes easy coverage. Positive-generation coverage and active-bound fractions are therefore reported separately. The rapid-change definition uses actual target-to-previous-ten-minute generation changes above 200.001 kW, exclusively for scoring.

The adaptive floors bind 86.97% of margins and the inherited gap fallback never activates during evaluation. Signed versus absolute scores, fixed versus rolling updating and floor magnitudes change together, so the original benefit cannot be attributed solely to adaptation. The floors' historical derivation is unresolved. Fixed-alpha rolling calibration is not adaptive-alpha conformal inference. Dependence, shift, floors and fallback prevent borrowing exchangeability-based guarantees. Coverage is horizonwise, not conditional or simultaneous across six horizons. A one-sided set is `[lower, +infinity)` with infinite width; nominal-minus-lower is a finite downside offset. The separately scored fixed absolute-error two-sided interval is a different method. See [coverage tables](../results/research_grade_v1/forecast_uncertainty/coverage_horizon_metrics.csv) and [rapid-event coverage](../results/research_grade_v1/forecast_uncertainty/coverage_rapid_change_metrics.csv).

## Control and physical interpretation

Both frozen controllers worsen original aggregate violations and summed exceedance against uncontrolled wind: uncontrolled has 1,465 violations, fixed 1,502 and adaptive 1,486. Adaptive improves on fixed by 27.86% in throughput, 0.77% in exceedance and sixteen violations; this does not establish net grid-ramp mitigation. Maximum ramps improve while average ramps worsen. The threshold is a study setting, not a verified site-specific grid requirement.

Two scenario paths are not a complete uncertainty distribution. Later scenario-dependent plans approximate recourse; the 100 kW allowance relaxes the primary horizon objective. Feasible SOC and power balance do not guarantee realised ramp compliance. Constant efficiencies, ideal wind-limited execution, no degradation/thermal/network model, finite horizon and daily SOC resets restrict realism. Lower throughput is a usage proxy, not demonstrated lifetime improvement. Hypothetical terminal-adjusted export is inventory accounting, not actual export or feasible subsequent dispatch. A perfect-forecast composite MPC baseline is nondeployable and is not a mathematical bound on every realised outcome.

## Statistical and computational limits

The 7,056 intervals and 42,336 horizon pairs are dependent observations. Supplementary paired, within-segment, non-circular moving-block resampling uses 10,000 draws, seed 20261029 and all declared lengths of two, three and seven episodes. Approximate recentered basic intervals assume adequate local stationarity and dependence approximation; they cannot remove selection bias or restore confirmatory independence. At length seven, the five-episode first segment has no resampling variation.

Relative throughput intervals remain below zero at all three lengths, while exceedance and violation intervals cross zero. For example, the two-episode approximate 95% intervals are −38.39% to −17.29% for throughput and −1.85% to +0.29% for exceedance. These are conditional exploratory uncertainty statements, not significance or equivalence claims. Publish every declared length, including the shorter-variation warning in [resampling limits](../results/research_grade_v1_20261029/statistics_original/resampling_segment_limits.csv) and the [complete intervals](../results/research_grade_v1_20261029/statistics_original/dependent_intervals.csv).

Original traces omit full plans, timings and solver certificates. Newly instrumented replays cannot be described as original logs or field deployment. A successful local test does not prove a clean clone contains licensed data, models and outputs; acquisition, release packaging and independent environment reproduction remain necessary.
