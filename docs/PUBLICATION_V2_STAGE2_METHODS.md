# Publication v2 Stage 2: forecasting and uncertainty methods

Prepared 1 November 2026. This document specifies the new **development-only** forecasting/uncertainty implementation. The [Stage 1 report](PUBLICATION_V2_STAGE1_REPORT.md), [chronology protocol](PUBLICATION_V2_PROTOCOL.md), [original audit](RESEARCH_AUDIT.md) and [gated Stage 2 plan](PUBLICATION_V2_STAGE2_PLAN.md) remain unchanged. The actual technical report must distinguish implemented interfaces, completed software tests, executed research experiments and blocked experiments.

**`BLOCKED_CONFIRMATORY_TEST` remains in force.** No local historical interval is redesignated untouched. New development training is also refused when its own required event/availability/provenance evidence is absent. Permission to develop adapters does not supply missing data evidence. Synthetic fixtures may test real libraries and numerical algorithms, but cannot supply research measurements or model-comparison findings.

## Experimental identity and target

The three model families are persistence, XGBoost and LightGBM, with direct forecasts at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 minutes. Every model consumes the same validated batch, fourteen ordered feature names, origin identifiers and six horizon definitions. Persistence deliberately uses only the current-power component and repeats it across the six horizons; this is the definition of the baseline, not a different observation cohort. Each booster fits one regressor per horizon. No neural model or new MPC controller belongs to this stage.

The common target is nonnegative available wind power in kW, with any clipping transformation declared before fitting and applied identically to training labels and scored observations. The repository's signed raw current-power features and nonnegative physical target convention must remain explicit. Changing a target convention creates a new experiment identity rather than replacing historical results.

The initial experiment configuration fixes a chronological training window, separate calibration window and later development-scoring window before outcomes are examined. All six fitting target labels must be eligible; every model receives the same training origins and labels. Static partitions satisfy the Stage 1 all-horizon target-disjointness and strict embargo rules. Persistence has no fitted statistical parameters but retains the same training-window identity for comparability. A missing library is an explicit dependency blocker, not permission to omit a comparator from an ostensibly complete three-model comparison.

Each saved experiment records seed, source/config/data hashes, feature order, split origins and target boundaries, clipping policy, hyperparameters, Python and package versions, model hashes, fitting availability and run status. Library defaults that affect a fit are recorded through the effective configuration. Single-thread execution and fixed seeds reduce numerical variation; neither alone proves byte-identical behaviour across arbitrary software versions or machines. Native save/reload and repeated-fit checks belong to software verification.

Stage 2 has its own [dependency declaration](../publication_v2/stage2/requirements.txt). LightGBM **4.7.0** was added to the local environment without upgrading its dependencies; old model/data/source/manifest bytes and older requirement files remain preserved. This legitimate environment change can make the earlier Stage 1 audit's strict current-environment comparison fail. It does not make Stage 1 evidence disappear or authorize rewriting its recorded environment. Stage 2 records its own environment and source identity; installed-package capture still does not establish an external clean-clone reproduction.

The ledger's data-clock fitting cutoff and the artifact's actual wall-clock fitting timestamp are different records. A retrospective replay may test a declared past-information window, but it does not prove that the newly fitted model existed at a historical issue time or met a real training/decision deadline. Do not substitute filesystem modification time for either record. Operational commissioning and computation latency require additional evidence before Stage 3 makes any deployment-timing claim.

## Information and provenance gates

A model adapter's low-level numeric `fit` method is not an admission gate for research data. Production fitting must occur only after the dataset/provenance layer has validated the complete record. A numeric matrix, current file modification time or plausible-looking timestamp cannot establish historical availability.

The Stage 1 contracts apply without weakening their strict target inequality:

1. Every source event, receipt, feature availability, origin, issuance, decision, target, model-fitting and observation/residual availability timestamp is present and compatible with an attested clock convention. Unresolved historical timestamps are never silently localized to UTC.
2. A feature enumerates its full source support. Required lag timestamps are exact joins; missing records, duplicate timestamps, centered windows, future source observations and undeclared gap repairs fail. Every source receipt precedes feature availability, and feature availability is no later than the origin.
3. Forecast target time is origin plus its named horizon. Origin is no later than issuance, issuance is no later than decision, and decision strictly precedes the target. Model training targets precede origins; the fitted model must already be available when it is issued.
4. Training and preprocessing can use only permitted training roles, with target labels and required receipts available by the declared fit time. Later calibration/development outcomes cannot influence fitting, early stopping, feature selection or hyperparameter selection.
5. A calibration residual retains the original model/forecast identity and satisfies **`target_time < decision_time`**, plus observation and residual computation availability no later than the current decision. Equal-time labels remain excluded even if a current observation could be a valid current-state feature.
6. Outcome scoring is a separate phase after a prediction/interval has been sealed and its target released. Future target values are not passed into prediction or interval construction. Rapid-event membership is an offline scoring stratum, not a forecast input.

The complete data identity includes source/release version, acquisition record, licence/use permission, units, measurement-window semantics, timezone/DST/clock synchronization, missing-data policy and availability lineage. Recorded SHA256 values authenticate bytes; they do not fabricate any of these facts. Historical fitting provenance for the frozen models remains unestablished. New fitted models acquire their own provenance and never overwrite the seven historical model artifacts.

## Fair point-forecast scoring

Scoring uses the same origin-target identifiers and observations for all three models. A failed model, missing prediction or missing target fails the requested comparison; it is never silently removed through a model-specific complete-case mask. Any deliberate common exclusion policy is fixed in advance and saved with counts and reasons.

For each horizon, record sample count, MAE, RMSE and signed bias in kW. Compute aggregate RMSE from mean squared errors rather than averaging episode RMSEs. Report persistence-relative differences with the sign convention explicit; improvement is an observed outcome, not a report-generation assertion. XGBoost or LightGBM need not outperform persistence.

Rapid events use the actual change from the target's preceding ten-minute observation to its actual target observation, with the predeclared threshold recorded in the configuration. These are post-outcome descriptive strata. Save rapid-event counts and errors at each horizon, and label an absent stratum undefined rather than zero error. Overlapping horizon targets and serial weather dependence do not form independent replicates. Stage 2 descriptive comparisons do not imply statistical significance or fresh independent validation.

## Horizon-specific calibration

For model identity `m` and horizon `h`, the symmetric absolute-error score is `s_i = |y_i - prediction_i|` in kW. Calibration never pools horizons or substitutes another model's residuals. Static calibration uses a separately declared fixed set; an unavailable record in that fixed set is a failed boundary, not an implicitly shortened static experiment. Rolling methods explicitly select strictly eligible records before applying the declared window.

For an unweighted eligible set of size `n`, the finite-sample order statistic uses `k = ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))`, with no interpolation. Insufficient support for a finite requested order statistic is reported explicitly; it does not silently produce zero uncertainty. A static radius and a rolling radius share this score definition, while their admitted histories differ.

A recency-weighted alternative selects the same last eligible window as rolling calibration, then uses `w_i = 2^(-(d-target_i)/half_life)` with ages and half-life in minutes. The test point has weight one and score `+infinity`. Its finite radius is the first ordered residual score whose cumulative weight reaches `(1-alpha)*(sum(w_i)+1)`; if finite calibration mass cannot reach that level, calibration fails explicitly. Effective sample size is `(sum(w_i))^2/sum(w_i^2)` and is descriptive, not a count of independent observations. Weighted or rolling time-series adaptation is a development heuristic unless the needed distributional assumptions are established; a finite-sample rank formula or a test-point mass alone is not a time-series coverage theorem. All weights, the window, half-life and insufficient-support rule are fixed before scoring outcomes.

The two-sided nonnegative-target interval is `[max(0, prediction-q), prediction+q]` after applying the declared optional radius floor. A configured floor is separately identified and is not derived from scoring outcomes. The base Stage 2 floor must not inherit the historically unresolved signed-floor provenance by implication. Record both the unconstrained radius and applied margin. In the implementation, `floor_binding` means `floor_kw > 0 and floor_kw >= unfloored_quantile`; positive-floor ties count as binding even when they do not increase the radius. A floor is not evidence of adaptation or guaranteed coverage. If a lower-only uncertainty object is exported for later MPC, it is labelled `[L,+infinity)` and has infinite width; its finite downside offset is not an interval width.

Publish static, rolling and weighted alternatives on the same model/decision/outcome cohort. The current predeclared uncertainty ablation compares static history, rolling windows of 72/144/288 eligible residuals, and weighted alternatives with 720/1,440-minute half-lives, retaining the same forecasts and scoring records. All current variants explicitly disable the floor (`floor_kw=0`); their floor-binding rate is therefore zero by configuration, not evidence against or for a positive floor. Positive-floor behaviour is software-tested, while a scientific positive-floor ablation would require a separately declared configuration and eligible observations. Do not pick an alternative because it performs best on the development scores and call it a confirmed improvement. A missing eligible history creates a recorded failure/blocker rather than a comparator-specific deletion.

The weighted-quantile motivation is consistent with [Barber, Candès, Ramdas and Tibshirani, *Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415), also published with [DOI 10.1214/23-AOS2276](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2276). That work analyses coverage loss through distributional comparisons. This project has not estimated those quantities or established its assumptions for the wind series, so citing the construction does not certify this temporal weighting rule or its conditional ramp-event coverage.

## Coverage, drift and figures

For each model, horizon and calibration method, report interval sample count, marginal empirical coverage, rapid-event coverage, mean interval width in kW, coverage minus the nominal reference, and floor-binding fraction. Report conditional-event sample sizes and undefined values honestly. Drift summaries use chronological periods fixed before outcome inspection, with both coverage and width; they do not search for a flattering calendar cut.

Coverage is horizonwise and descriptive. Marginal coverage does not establish conditional coverage during ramps, simultaneous six-horizon coverage, exchangeability or robust control. Weighted/rolling adaptation, distribution shift, dependent residuals and repeated targets further limit coverage interpretation. No confidence interval or significance statement is invented when no corresponding dependence-aware calculation was performed.

Original research figures are generated only from completed eligible development outputs: common actual/predicted traces, horizon error comparisons, interval examples, coverage/width/drift diagnostics and uncertainty ablations. A blocked study instead exports machine-readable blocker/status tables and an explicitly labelled protocol/readiness figure; it does not draw fictional model errors or coverage curves. Figures from synthetic fixtures, if retained, are software-test demonstrations only and are excluded from research comparisons.

## Completion and interpretation

New outputs occupy unique versioned `publication_v2/` paths. Existing or partial paths are never resumed by trusting CSV existence. A completed run authenticates source/config/environment/input/model/output identities and is distinguishable from a blocked preflight or software-test run. Preservation checks include the Stage 1 files as well as frozen data/models/results. No merge or push follows from local completion.

The archived negative result remains visible: uncontrolled wind had 1,465 violations and 317,210.288 kW summed exceedance, versus fixed-margin MPC's 1,502/326,158.296 and adaptive one-sided MPC's 1,486/323,638.526. Those are previously archived retrospective control scores, not outcomes of Stage 2 forecasting. Better forecast or interval metrics, if later observed, would not themselves establish battery or grid benefits. The [Stage 3 integration specification](PUBLICATION_V2_STAGE3_MPC_SPEC.md) therefore requires a separate guarded control evaluation.
